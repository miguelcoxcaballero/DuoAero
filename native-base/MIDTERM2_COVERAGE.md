# Second-midterm coverage and release plan

The course-outline order is launch vehicles, space-flight environment,
space vehicles, the topic-4 introduction, diverse aircraft and rotorcraft.
The items below are a finite review checklist, not a claim that a section is
complete. The current development release-shaped path includes the three
space nodes; no 2.0 release is authorized by this file.

The space nodes use stable `second-partial-<topic>-lesson-<index>` IDs and
their existing stable level and skill IDs. No published first-midterm proof
covers these new exercises; no migration, completion, gems or XP is granted for them.
The release-shaped diagnostic checks those invariants before walking the path.
Production publication remains blocked on the complete course, original UI,
progress/reward behavior, animation and update acceptance described elsewhere.

A strict diagnostic is prepared for the current base lessons of topics 4,
5.1 and 5.2, using their actual original path nodes and requiring native
positive feedback plus saved completion after restart. It seeds the earlier
diagnostic sessions and therefore cannot establish an unseeded whole-course
traversal. These current base versions still need that Android run.

A third 3.3 mission-typology lesson is now authored as an isolated draft:
flybys, orbiters and occultations, atmospheric probes and balloons, penetrators,
rovers and space stations. The selected distinctions were checked against the
provided course material. Its ten native-model questions preserve unique IDs;
ordering, actual path integration, Android grading and visual review are still
pending, and it grants no completion or rewards.

A third 3.2 environment draft covers buoyancy-driven convection in
microgravity, crew and EVA effects, vacuum-sensitive materials, radiation
mitigation, thermal stress, radioisotope electrical power, water recycling,
oxygen production and navigation clocks. It is not yet connected to the
path or credited; its native interaction, source editorial review beyond the
selected course material and visual layout are pending.

A separate source check covers five of the new environment questions:
ENV-021 (ventilation), ENV-027 (radioisotope generation), ENV-028–029
(water recovery and electrolysis), and ENV-030 (navigation clocks). Public
references: [ESA ventilation](https://blogs.esa.int/astronauts/2012/04/29/no-molecule-shall-stand-still/),
[NASA life support](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/environmental-control-and-life-support-systems-eclss/),
[NASA radioisotope power](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160001769/downloads/20160001769.pdf),
and [NIST clocks](https://www.nist.gov/atomic-clocks/a-powerful-tool-for-science/putting-einstein-test).
The water item does not assert a fixed recovery percentage. This partial source
check does not accept the other five questions, full coverage or Android UI.

The remaining five draft items have also been compared with the supplied
course outline: ENV-022 concerns reduced loading and deconditioning; ENV-023
concerns EVA tether attachment; ENV-024 concerns vacuum-sensitive materials;
ENV-025 concerns radiation mitigation; ENV-026 concerns thermal deformation.
Their distractors and exact wording still require classroom-level editorial
and Android review before the lesson is connected.

The other five environment concepts have an independent public-source check:

These checks do not establish course completeness or Android acceptance.
